The shift

Every assistant so far
has waited to be asked.

You ask, it answers, and then it forgets you until next time. That was never what a chief of staff did. A great one knows what you promised, notices what’s due, and brings you only the two things that truly need you.

  • Tuesday.“I’ll send it over tonight.” Said in a meeting. Forgotten by Friday.
  • Friday.The school form, due today, under forty newsletters.
  • 11:48 pm.Still answering, filing and chasing, instead of living.

On your Mac

Talk to it
like a person.

Ask out loud, or type. It answers from your own mail, messages and files, in a sentence, and tells you where the answer came from. No forms, no settings, no dashboards to keep up with.

  • The morning talk.

    What needs you today, then the few questions only you can answer. Never the same one twice. Stop whenever you like.

  • Only what needs you.

    Today holds at most three lines, each with why. Not a feed, not a count, not another inbox.

  • Your word wins.

    Tell it once, in a sentence: “remember, Rahul is my co-founder”. Nothing it reads can overrule you.

  • The people who matter.

    Learned from how you actually live, on your Mac: who you call, who you answer first. Not from your address book.

Nothing that matters slips.
Nothing that doesn’t reaches you.

Questions

Before you ask.

Do I need a Mac?

Yes. Early access runs on a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or later) on macOS 26. The Mac is where Anthrie can read your mail, messages and calls in place, with your permission.

Does my data leave my Mac?

No. In early access everything stays on your Mac, encrypted, and it hears you on your Mac alone. No server of ours sees your data. How it handles your data, in plain words.

Will it send anything for me?

Not in early access: it only reads. It never sends, pays, signs or deletes. Drafts and follow-ups come later, one kind of task at a time and only with your say.
